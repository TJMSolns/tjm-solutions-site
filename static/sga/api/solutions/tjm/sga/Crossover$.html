def multiPointCrossover[A](pc: Double, points: Int): () => Vector[A]

Multi-point crossover: with probability pc, cut at points distinct random loci and alternate segments between the parents; otherwise the offspring are copies of their parents.

Multi-point crossover: with probability pc, cut at points distinct random loci and alternate segments between the parents; otherwise the offspring are copies of their parents.

Deliberately not implemented in terms of singlePointCrossover — sampling points distinct cut positions needs shuffle, which draws far more randomness than a single nextInt call. Routing the points = 1 case through that machinery would be correct but wasteful, and would silently change singlePointCrossover's documented draw sequence. Two strategies for two different GA configurations, not the same logic duplicated.

def orderCrossover[A](pc: Double): () => Vector[A]

Order crossover for permutation genomes (TSP's tour ordering, or any other "search over orderings" representation) -- singlePoint/ multiPointCrossover splice two segments from two DIFFERENT permutations, which produces a Vector with duplicate/missing elements, not a permutation; this representation genuinely needs its own Crossover. With probability pc: pick two random cut points, keep one parent's segment between them fixed at its original position, then fill the remaining positions left-to-right with the OTHER parent's elements in their own relative order, skipping anything already placed by the segment. A real, well-known technique in GA literature (commonly called order crossover / OX) -- described here by its actual mechanics rather than pinned to a specific paper's exact procedure (no primary source checked directly, same caution shuffledMultiPointCrossover's own docstring correction already established after a prior fabricated attribution).

def shuffledMultiPointCrossover[A](pc: Double, points: Int): () => Vector[A]

Shuffle crossover (Eshelman, Caruana & Schaffer 1989, via GEATbx) combined with multi-point crossover — permute the loci of both parents with the same random permutation, run multiPointCrossover on the permuted genomes, then invert the permutation on the offspring. Removes the positional bias plain multi-point crossover has (loci near each other tend to travel together across generations) without changing what "a cut" or "a segment" means.

Shuffle crossover (Eshelman, Caruana & Schaffer 1989, via GEATbx) combined with multi-point crossover — permute the loci of both parents with the same random permutation, run multiPointCrossover on the permuted genomes, then invert the permutation on the offspring. Removes the positional bias plain multi-point crossover has (loci near each other tend to travel together across generations) without changing what "a cut" or "a segment" means.

The technique is Eshelman, Caruana & Schaffer's shuffle crossover. It is not PCKaNN's crossover: neither PCKaNN source (Moores 1998; Moores 2000) describes a shuffle step, and the thesis's complete list of reproduction operators (Xover1/Xover2..., single and multi-point) doesn't include one. PCKaNN's actual crossover is plain multiPointCrossover, built separately.

def singlePointCrossover[A](pc: Double): () => Vector[A]

Mitchell's single-point crossover: with probability pc, splice the parents at a random locus; otherwise the offspring are copies of their parents.

Mitchell's single-point crossover: with probability pc, splice the parents at a random locus; otherwise the offspring are copies of their parents.

Parameterized over the allele type A, not any one representation — reusable across every genome that happens to be Vector[A] (bit strings, integer-coded, real-valued, ...). A representation that cannot tolerate arbitrary splicing (permutations, trees) needs its own Crossover instance; that's a genuinely new representation, not a reason to generalize this one further.
